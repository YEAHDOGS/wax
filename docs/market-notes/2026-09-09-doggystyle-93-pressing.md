## Why this matters for Wax

**Condition drives a 5–10x price spread on the same release.** The median
original pressing ($204) sits ~5x above a sealed reissue ($40), and within
the original pressing itself, beat-up copies ($89) vs near-mint ones
($618) span ~7x. Two product implications:

1. A price alert that ignores pressing/condition is noise — "Doggystyle at
   $35" is a reissue, not a deal on an original. Wax's alert engine must
   key on release identity (Discogs release ID / catalog #), not just
   artist+title.
2. The spread itself is a signal: a sudden sub-$100 original-pressing
   listing in VG+ condition is the exact "steal" a collector wants pinged
   about. That's a percentile-vs-condition alert, not a flat price alert.

## Caveats

- Marketplace stats from a search index, not the live Discogs API —
   numbers may lag the live marketplace by weeks/months.
- Asking prices, not sold prices. Sold-history would be the truer value
   signal (candidate Wax data source: Discogs sold-listings when/if API
   access is available).
- eBay listing is one data point (letitspin, $135 + shipping), not a
   market average.
